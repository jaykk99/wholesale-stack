Wholesaling Playbook

How to find deals, lock them up, and get paid.

1. Finding Motivated Sellers

Driving for Dollars

Drive through target neighborhoods and spot neglected homes: overgrown lawns, boarded windows, code violation notices, vacant properties. Trace the owners and reach out.

List Building and Data

Use real estate data platforms like DealMachine, BatchLeads, or PropStream to pull high-equity, pre-foreclosure, probate, tax-delinquent, or absentee-owner lists.

Cold Calling and Direct Mail

Contact distressed owners directly by phone, text campaigns, or targeted postcards offering a fast, all-cash solution.

Classifieds and Local Ads

Post or search ads on Craigslist or Facebook Marketplace to find private owners looking to offload properties quickly.

2. Locking Up the Deal

Contract Assignment (Most Common)

Sign a Purchase and Sale Agreement with an assignment clause. Transfer your right to buy to an end buyer for an assignment fee. You never own the property.

Double Closing

Two simultaneous transactions: buy from the owner, then immediately sell to your cash buyer. Useful when assignment clauses are restricted, like bank-owned properties.

Wholesaling via LLC

Form a dedicated LLC to act as the buyer on the contract, then sell the LLC membership interests to the investor instead of assigning the contract.

3. Finding Cash Buyers

REIA Meetings

Attend local or virtual Real Estate Investor Association meetups. They are full of active fix-and-flip investors and landlords looking for deals.

Cash Buyer Search

Search public county records for recent all-cash sales. Google local "we buy houses" sites to find active buyers in your market.

Social Media

Post deal breakdowns, repair estimates, and ARV numbers on Instagram and Facebook. Buyers come to you when they see the math.

The Simple Math

Your profit = Contract price with buyer - Price you locked in with seller

Example: lock it at $80,000, assign to a buyer at $95,000, keep the $15,000 spread.

Quick check: Max Offer = (ARV x 0.70) - repair costs. If the seller takes less than that, there is room for your fee.

How to Sell a Dead Person's House (Probate)

When an owner passes away, the heirs often want cash fast instead of managing a house. These are some of the best deals.

Finding Them

Watch obituaries and probate court filings. Look for vacant houses with overgrown lawns and piled-up mail. Ask neighbors. Skip-trace the heirs.

What to Say

Be human first. "I'm sorry for your loss. I help families who inherited a house they don't want to deal with. I can make a fast cash offer and handle everything." Use the cold call script, but lead with sympathy, not price.

What Contract

Start with the purchase agreement, then assign it to a cash buyer for your fee. Heirs usually care about speed and simplicity, not top dollar.

Watch Out For

Make sure the person signing has the legal right to sell (executor or court approval). If multiple heirs disagree, walk away.

How to Sell a House That Can't Pay for Itself (Pre-Foreclosure)

When an owner falls behind on mortgage payments, they need out before the bank takes it. Speed is everything.

Finding Them

Pre-foreclosure lists from PropStream or BatchLeads. Notice of default filings at the county. Houses with foreclosure notices posted on the door.

What to Say

"I see you're behind on payments. I can close fast with cash before the foreclosure hits your record." Urgency helps them, not pressure. Use the cold call script, then the objection handlers for timing.

What Contract

Use the purchase agreement with a fast closing date. These deals move in days, not weeks. Have your buyer lined up before you sign.

Watch Out For

Check how much is owed vs what the house is worth. If they owe more than it's worth, there is no spread and no deal.
